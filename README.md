# unijack-3d

A rotatable, explodable, photo-anchored 3D model of the UniJack kit (base board, Mercury module, PeriBoard) that runs in the
browser with three.js. **It is illustrative, not CAD.**

## Run it

From this folder (macOS or Linux):

```
python3 -m http.server 4173
```

Open <http://127.0.0.1:4173/>. No build step and no network: three.js r186 is vendored under `vendor/` and the viewer uses system fonts.
It is ES modules with an import map, so it has to be served over http (double-clicking `index.html` will not work).

## What you can do

* Drag to rotate, scroll or pinch to zoom, right-drag or two-finger drag to pan. Keyboard: arrow keys rotate, `+` / `-` zoom.
* **Auto-rotate** on/off, and **Exploded view** (the boards separate straight up along their mating axis, dashed guides trace each
  board back to its seat, the socket latches spring open, name tags appear). Both work together. A slider scrubs the explosion.
* **Mercury insertion**: scrub or play an SO-DIMM-style insertion (enters at an angle, then presses down; the latches click shut).
* Camera views (Hero, Top, Low, Side, Underside), per-board visibility, board designator labels, and a searchable **Parts** list that
  flies the camera to a part. Click any part for an info card with an honesty tag (photo-verified, photo-derived, assumed).

## How it is built (and what is and is not real)

The board tops are the **real gallery photographs**, so silkscreen, copper and text are real. On top of the photo, every identifiable
part is extruded from its measured footprint with typical heights and real-shaped detail (leads, pins, latch clips, contacts). The photo
is projected straight down onto every part's top face, so each part looks like its photographed self from above and has real depth
from any other angle.

| item | status |
|---|---|
| Board sizes: base about 160 x 87 mm, PeriBoard about 45 x 87 mm, Mercury about 35.5 x 69.6 mm | photo-derived, each within a few percent. **Each board is scaled from standard-pitch features on that board**, because the flat-lay photo is a composite of separately scaled cut-outs: the base from the lead pitch of a 0.5 mm quad-flat IC (its photo is stretched about 11 percent in x: 8.82 px/mm along x, 7.94 along z), the PeriBoard from the 0.635 mm pitch of its connectors (17.57 px/mm, square), Mercury from its 0.5 mm contact pitch (measures 0.498) and its 69.6 mm length (round mounting pads measure round). Independent check: after rectification the three mating connector pairs between the base and the PeriBoard agree in length to about 1.5 percent and in position to 0.4 mm, and the two boards come out the same depth. Mercury's 35.5 mm width is taller than the 30 mm JEDEC SO-DIMM; no Mercury drawing was checked. Tools: `tools/pitch-check.py`. |
| Part footprints and positions | photo-derived (overlay-checked against the photos, see `tools/overlay.py`; most within about 0.5 mm) |
| Designators (J100, J400, ...) and chip markings | photo-verified: the printed text is legible in a photo. The part's shape is photo-derived and its height assumed. |
| Part heights, plastic and metal colours | **assumed**. Matched-camera comparisons with the 3/4 photo suggest some tall parts (RJ45, barrel jack, terminal) are lower than typical values; treat all heights as plus or minus 30 percent. |
| Detail geometry: connector contacts, IC leads, latch hooks, solder balls, pin tips | synthetic, built to look right; counts come from the photos where legible. The PeriBoard headers J1-J16 and the base headers J305-J307 are modelled with **vertical** pins: the flat-lay photos (gallery-2/3, the textures) show the pin tips end-on as two columns of small gold squares inside each body and nothing lying on the board beside them; in the 3/4 photo (gallery-1) the pins appear to lie to the right about one body-width long, but so does every vertical edge at that side of the frame (the base's J305-J307 pins and the board edge lean the same way), and the J16 pin tips reach past the board edge, which a pin lying on the board could not. Pin heights stay assumed (see `qa-out/geometry/crops`). J305-J307: gallery-1 shows the pins standing well clear of a low shroud, so the shroud is modelled low (about 2.6 mm) with pins to about 5.4 mm, both assumed. |
| Micro-geometry: rounded edges, solder fillets, chamfered pin tips, capacitor rim | **assumed**. The photos show softly rounded housing, shell and IC-body edges, bright concave reflowed solder at every gull-wing foot and header tail, square header pins with pointed tips, and a bevelled rim on the electrolytic can, but none of these can be measured in them. The model adds a uniform 0.2 mm edge bevel (0.3 mm on housings taller than 4 mm, a half-round on thin shell tops) to every free-standing box part taller than 2 mm, a toe and heel fillet of typical size (at most 0.3 mm long, at most 1.1 x the lead width) at every gull-wing lead, header tail and contact foot, a 15 percent tip taper on the header pins and a 0.4 mm chamfer on the can. Every toe fillet sits inside the photo-traced lead or pad extent (the foot is shortened by the fillet length), so no lead reaches further than it does in the photo. Edges where one moulded piece is built from several boxes are left sharp so the surface stays continuous; stamped steel parts (latch clips, tabs, pads) are left sharp. Board edges are plain FR4 as in gallery-1 (no plated edge is visible, none is modelled). |
| PeriBoard stack height above the base | **assumed** (not published; the photos do not pin it down) |
| PCB thickness (1.6 mm base and PeriBoard, about 1.2 mm Mercury) | **assumed** |
| Insertion angle (28 degrees), slide distance, latch travel, explode distances | **assumed** and illustrative (take them from Jens's insertion video) |
| Texture patch under the socket latches | **synthetic**. The flat-lay photo has the closed latch arms baked in; while the latches are open a small patch cloned from a plain region of the board covers those pixels. It is hidden whenever the latches are closed. |
| Surface finish (glossy solder mask, matte silkscreen, metallic pads, shields and gold, matte IC bodies and markings) | **photo-derived by colour segmentation**, not measured. `tools/derive-maps.py` classifies every texture pixel by hue / saturation / value (PIL HSV 0..255: mask = S above 70..110 and hue 112..175; silkscreen = S below 45..100 and V above 178..205; tin = low S with V between; dark = low S and V below 75..105; gold = hue 4..50 and S above 55..95) and then applies three morphological rules: a white blob enclosed by tin is the highlight on a shiny pad (tin, not silkscreen); a thin tin band on a silkscreen / mask boundary is the anti-aliased edge of a white glyph (silkscreen, so glyphs get no metallic outline); and pale pixels enclosed by a dark body (the holes of the dark mask after a 2 px closing) are a matte **marking** class (roughness 0.60, no metal, no relief): the laser markings on the Infineon, Lattice, FTDI and LVC packages, and highlights and tongues inside black housings. It writes roughness / metalness maps (mask 0.45 dielectric, silk 0.78, tin 0.32 metallic, dark 0.62, marking 0.60, gold 0.30 metallic on Mercury and a hedged 0.40 / half-metal on the base and PeriBoard, where tan connector plastic is colour-identical to gold plating) plus a mild **synthetic** normal map (silkscreen proud, pads below the mask; markings and bodies flat). The maps are stored as lossless WebP (they are data: the file holds the 8-bit map exactly, R = 255 in `-rm`; an earlier lossy encoding put errors of up to 0.48 into the metalness channel) and travel in the GLB as PNG. Every map has a provenance sidecar `<map>.webp.json` (tool and git commit, source texture sha256, every threshold, class fractions, disclosure); class fractions per texture (base-top: mask 60, tin 13, silk 9, dark 12, gold 3, marking 1.7 percent) in `assets/tex/derived/README.md`. Limits: a white pixel is treated as silkscreen whether it is silkscreen or white plastic, and a grey pixel as metal whether it is a pad or a grey part: the grey plastic housing of the J400 SO-DIMM socket, the largest grey part in the Hero view, reads as metal; the pale bevel ring of some IC bodies reads as metal; pin tips inside black header shrouds get the matte marking finish (they are covered by the pin geometry); a steel part photographed dark in the flat lay (the USB-A J304 shell top) is classed dark and renders as matte black, although gallery-1.jpg shows bright steel. (An earlier version classed the IC markings as tin, so they rendered as cyan mirror text; the marking class fixes that.) Metal pixels use the photo's own colour times 1.2 as their specular colour (`PHOTO_METAL_F0`, **assumed**: the photographed grey of a pad is its reflection of the photographer's softbox, not its reflectance; 1.5 was tried and over-brightened the pads against the gallery-4 macro; the viewer's gain is a diffuse calibration and is not applied to metals; the GLB carries the plain photo colour). Under the studio rig the pads carry a slight cool cast (Mercury close-up pad mean sRGB about 182/202/215 against the macro's neutral 168/165/167): that is the colour of the rig's cool fill, rim and walls, which the metal mirrors, not the maps, and the F0 factor does not change it. A "delight" pass that flattens the photos' large-scale luminance was measured and rejected: in these evenly lit flat lays that variation is the copper under the mask, not the lighting. |
| Lighting and colour | The viewer relights the photographs. **Calibration target: `gallery-1.jpg`** (the 3/4 photograph), not the flat-lay textures. The photo material's diffuse gain (`PHOTO_GAIN` 0.70) and the studio's light energy are set so that in the Hero preset (1600 x 900, az 32, el 34, post chain and depth of field on) the plain solder-mask boxes 586,380-602,388 and 556,403-574,414, which correspond to the photo's patches A (photo box 800,790-940,835, mean sRGB 12/151/189) and B (690,855-780,915, 4/132/170), render at 16/150/196 and 18/127/167: **1.008 and 0.927 of the photo's linear luminance** (luminance of each box's mean colour), green/blue 0.77 and 0.76 against the photo's 0.78-0.80 (the texture's own hue at patch A is 0.75). On the white sweep (`?env=sweep`) the same boxes read 16/148/195 and 18/125/166, 0.981 and 0.903. This is the automated colour check: `tools/colorcal.py --hero <hero render>` passes when both ratios are within 0.90..1.10 (CONTRACT.md, Acceptance). The solder mask reflects the environment at 0.15 strength (`PHOTO_ENV`; metals reflect it fully): the studio map has a ceiling panel and a large key box that a glossy mask mirrors, the photos' lights were off-axis, and their plain mask carries almost no neutral specular floor (patch A has red 12 of 255; at 0.25 the Hero mask rendered red 25, at 0.15 red 16). (In three r186 `material.envMapIntensity` is overwritten by `scene.environmentIntensity` for any material without its own `envMap`, so this is done per pixel in the shader, and the same shader hook is re-attached when the material is cloned for the latch texture patch, which otherwise rendered 1.5 x too bright and grey while the latches were open; now it matches its neighbouring mask within 5 to 8 percent in the top view.) The model's white plastics sit at the photo's white level (about 0.8 linear), so the energy was not raised further; the rest of the match came from the photo gain. **The flat-lay texture is a darker exposure than gallery-1**: at the same two mask spots the texture reads 0.100 and 0.100 linear where gallery-1 reads 0.258 and 0.196, 2.0 to 2.6 x. The old top-down check (`tools/colorcal.py` without `--hero`, a `--only=base --view=top` render against the texture over every mask pixel) therefore measures 2.71 x the texture's mask luminance under this calibration (1.20 after dividing by the 2.25 mean exposure difference); it is kept as an informational figure and its former 0.95-1.05 target is withdrawn, because that target and the gallery-1 look cannot both be held (it would need a gain of about 0.26, which puts the Hero mask at 0.37 of the photo). History: before the materials calibration the studio alone (gain 0.41) reached 0.62 and 0.58 of the photo at the same boxes. Pads read as bright glints without clipping (the hero's pixels at 254-255 are about 206 specular pin points; mask and pad means are below 230). Hue and highlights remain approximate. |
| Studio, ground and post-processing | **Synthetic rendering choices, not the photo's set-up.** The light is a procedural studio map (`src/lib/stage.js`, presets in `ENVS`: a large warm key box above-front-left, a cool fill, a cool rim strip, a soft panel behind the kit in the mirror direction of the Hero camera so the glossy mask shows a sheen like the softbox sheen in gallery-1, and a graded room) rendered through PMREMGenerator, plus one warm shadow-casting directional key (4096 px map fitted to the kit along a fixed direction, so refits while exploding do not bend the light; 5-tap PCF, soft) and a weak cool rim. The key's direction follows gallery-1.jpg (header shadows fall to the lower right); its softness, colour temperature and the panel radiances are judged by eye and by the mask measurements above, not measured from the photo. Two backdrops, both inventions: the default **dark studio** (a dark satin table, a disc that fades into a graded dark backdrop; its grazing reflection is capped so the Side preset shows no horizon step, floor/backdrop luminance 1.17) and, with the **Backdrop** button or `?env=sweep`, a **white sweep** like the one the photos were shot on (an unlit near-white table and backdrop, drawn a little below paper white so the key's shadow and the contact darkening stay visible; its walls are bright so metals reflect something). The two deliver the same light to the board: the mean solder-mask colour differs by under 10 percent between them (checked by `tools/viewer-shots.mjs`, measured 2 percent in the QA hero render). The table reflects only the studio map, never the board. Post-processing (`src/lib/post.js`): GTAO ambient occlusion (3.5 mm radius, contact darkening under connectors and the module), SMAA, and in the Hero preset a subtle depth of field that is on only while nothing moves (no camera tween, auto-rotate, drag, damping, insertion or explode animation); tone mapping and sRGB happen in the OutputPass. AO and DOF switch off after about 20 slow frames (over 30 ms) and with `?quality=low`; `?quality=high` keeps them. The post chain adds 444 draw calls (445 with the depth of field; measured 1197 / 1198 against the kit's plain 753: the GTAO normal/depth pass over the scene plus the full-screen quads) on top of the kit's own, which are measured with `--post=0`. |
| Undersides of the base board and PeriBoard | **not photographed**, shown plain. Mercury's back is photographed and shown. |
| Offline Cycles stills (`renders/`, made by `tools/blender/render.py` from the exported GLB) | **synthetic studio**: the three area lights, gradient world, ground plane, camera and the material look (specular level and thin coat on the photo faces, whose roughness/metalness/normal come from the photo-derived maps above; neutral metals forced to conductors with a chosen matte 0.45 roughness floor, gold glossier; a 1.5 x metal F0 on the photo faces, which was the viewer's assumed `PHOTO_METAL_F0` when the pipeline was written and stays `render.py`'s default so the existing renders are reproducible, while the viewer itself uses 1.2 now, see the surface-finish row; `--photo-metal-f0 1.2` follows the viewer) are render choices, not the photographs' lighting or measured finishes. Exposure is matched so the **mean** luminance of two plain solder-mask patches equals the mean of the same patches in `gallery-1.jpg`; it is mean-only (the two patches individually differ by about 0.3 stops in opposite directions) and hue is not matched: both studios render the mask lighter and less saturated than the photo (patch A: photo sRGB 12/151/189, dark studio 43/136/168, white studio 61/135/164; the dark studio is the nearer), and the dark ground is mostly sRGB 60-85 with a fill reflection up to about 160 near the bottom corners. The calibration is pinned to the GLB's sha256 and refused for a different GLB. Every render has a sidecar with the model commit and dirty flag, GLB checksum, render.py blob hash, all settings, a per-material record of what was changed, the measured numbers and the disclosure sentence. Same geometry, same assumed heights and colours as the viewer. |
| Function of any part | inferred from shape only ("appears to be ...") |

Nothing in this model is a vendor-supplied mechanical specification. When Jens supplies a STEP file or the KiCad/Altium project, replace
the part builders with geometry converted from that data and keep the same metadata.

## Layout

```
index.html, src/main.js, src/viewer.css   the viewer
src/model.js                              assembles the kit; insertion, latches, explode, guides
src/parts/base.js (+ base-left.js, base-right.js), peri.js, mercury.js   the part builders
src/lib/                                  frame + planar UV, materials, primitives, stage lighting (studio map, shadow fit, ground), post-processing chain
assets/tex/*.webp                         rectified, mm-scaled photo textures (derived from the gallery photos)
assets/tex/derived/*.webp, README.md      roughness/metalness (-rm) and normal (-nrm) maps segmented from the textures by colour (lossless WebP), with the thresholds and class fractions
data/scale.json                           scale anchors and board sizes
tools/extract.py                          photos -> textures + scale.json   (needs source-photos/, pillow numpy scipy)
tools/derive-maps.py                      textures -> assets/tex/derived (surface-finish maps by colour segmentation; needs pillow numpy scipy)
tools/qa-render.mjs, overlay.py, crop.py  headless QA renders, footprint-vs-photo overlay, zoom-with-grid
tools/viewer-shots.mjs                    end-to-end viewer test (clicks the UI, screenshots, console errors)
tools/export-glb.mjs                      exports assets/unijack.glb (named nodes, metadata in glTF extras)
tools/blender/render.py                   offline photoreal stills with Blender Cycles from the GLB (see tools/blender/README.md)
renders/                                  Cycles stills + provenance sidecars (not committed)
CONTRACT.md                               rules for part builders
```

## Tools (need Node 20+)

```
npm install                                   # playwright-core, three (dev)
node tools/qa-render.mjs --only=all --suite   # standard QA views into qa-out/
#   oblique photo renders go through the same post chain as the viewer (GTAO + SMAA + OutputPass); --post=0 for a plain render,
#   --dof=1 for the Hero depth of field, --env=sweep for the white sweep (--env=room: the previous RoomEnvironment rig), --shadow=vsm to compare shadow filters,
#   --look=key:11,sheen:1.8,keyLight:3.2,... to override the stage preset for calibration sweeps; the QA frameMs figure includes GPU completion (gl.finish + readPixels)
node tools/viewer-shots.mjs                   # drive the UI, save screenshots, report errors
node tools/export-glb.mjs                     # write assets/unijack.glb (carries the derived roughness/metalness and normal maps)
python3 tools/derive-maps.py                  # regenerate assets/tex/derived after the textures change (python with pillow, numpy, scipy)
python3 tools/colorcal.py --hero qa-out/x/hero.png   # colour acceptance: Hero render (--az=32 --el=34 --dof=1) vs gallery-1 mask patches, PASS within 0.90..1.10
python3 tools/colorcal.py qa-out/x/top.png           # informational: a `--only=base --view=top --mode=photo` render vs the (darker) flat-lay texture
<venv-bpy>/bin/python -I tools/blender/render.py --view hero --samples 192 --size 1920x1080   # Cycles still (needs the bpy wheel, see tools/blender/README.md)
```

The QA tools render with software WebGL (SwiftShader) so they run without a GPU, in a container or CI.
Note: on macOS, point Playwright at an installed Chromium/Chrome (`executablePath`) or run `npx playwright install chromium` once.

## Known limits

* Tiny passives (0402 and smaller) are texture-only; larger parts have real geometry.
* Real-time lighting cannot reproduce the full macro depth of field or the cinematic look of an offline render (the Hero preset's depth of field is a screen-space approximation). This is a viewer, not the
  promotional film; the film should be rendered offline from real CAD or from this GLB. `tools/blender/render.py` makes path-traced stills from the GLB with the viewer's camera presets (same az/el, fov and fit rule; `tools/blender/viewer-camera.mjs` checks the two cameras against each other, they agree to 0.01 mm at 1080p; about 7 to 9 minutes per 1080p hero frame on 4 CPU cores, 3 to 6 minutes for the other views, timed on the pre-integration GLB).
* Tall parts at the photo edge (RJ45, USB-A, barrel jack, terminal, mini- and micro-USB) show some of their own side faces in their top-face pixels (the flat lay is a perspective photo); their PCB-level footprints are displaced by a fraction of a millimetre.
* The satin table is a disc that fades out 200 to 520 mm from the kit into the backdrop; the white sweep is unlit, so its shadow is a fixed-opacity overlay rather than a lit surface. Post-processing (AO, SMAA, DOF) roughly doubles the frame cost; the adaptive quality drops AO and DOF on slow machines, and `?quality=low` turns the whole chain off.
* Size: the photo-derived finish maps are stored lossless (about 4 MB for the seven maps, against 1 MB when they were lossy, which corrupted the metalness channel), so the viewer fetches about 5 MB of textures (1.2 MB photos + 3.9 MB maps), the single-file bundle (`tools/bundle-single.mjs`) is about 8 MB (was 4.4) and the GLB about 17 MB (was 11) (its finish maps are PNG, the photo textures JPEG).
* Not yet tested on a real GPU, in Safari or on iOS.
